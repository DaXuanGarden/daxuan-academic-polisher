# English academic style

## Default register

Use clear international scientific English. If the user names Nature, Cell, Science, or a
specific journal, follow the supplied/current venue instructions; otherwise do not claim a
journal-house style. Preserve British or American spelling, person, and terminology from the
input.

Prefer concrete subjects and active verbs in Introduction, Results, and Discussion when the
author's voice permits it. Keep a conventional passive Methods section passive. Use past tense
for what the authors did and observed, and present tense for established knowledge, figures,
tables, and bounded implications.

## Review prompts

| Pattern to inspect | Plainer move when the source supports it |
| --- | --- |
| “It is worth noting”; “In recent years”; “In order to…” | open with the substance |
| “leverage”; “foster”; “unlock”; “showcase” | use the concrete action |
| “crucial”; “groundbreaking”; “comprehensive” | state the measured consequence or remove the empty adjective |
| “not merely X but Y”; stacked hedges; trailing participles | use one calibrated hedge or a separate sentence |
| “significant” without a statistical test; “novel/first” without support | use a magnitude or flag `[CHECK]` |

Avoid forced triads, repeated participial tails, identical openings, unnecessary em-dashes,
and paragraph-final recaps. Keep a term if it is field-standard or supported by the source.
Do not impose a fixed sentence-length quota or a generic “humanizer” voice.

## Integrity warning

Do not turn Chinese input into English unless the user explicitly requests English output. When
English output is explicitly requested, preserve numbers, citations, identifiers, and claim
strength; flag a standard English term that is uncertain rather than inventing one.
