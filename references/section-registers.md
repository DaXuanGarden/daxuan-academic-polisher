# Section and register rules

Use only rows that match the request. These are editing priorities, not permission to invent
content or alter a paper's structure.

| Section or register | Editing priority | Common boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abstract | Keep one compact evidence chain: problem/aim, approach, main finding, bounded conclusion. Respect the supplied limit and headings. | Do not add a result that appears only in the body. |
| Introduction | Move from the specific problem to the gap and aim. Keep the literature synthesis selective and critical. | Do not turn a language edit into a new literature review. |
| Methods | Make actors, actions, order, measurements, and conditions easy to follow. Preserve tense and passive convention. | Do not fill in missing protocol details. |
| Results | Put the finding first, retain quantitative support and uncertainty, and separate observation from interpretation. | Do not infer causality or add significance. |
| Discussion | Distinguish result, interpretation, limitation, and implication. Replace generic future work only when a named next step is supplied. | Do not replay every result or expand implications. |
| Conclusion | State the supported conclusion and its boundary in the shortest form. | Do not introduce new data or claims. |
| Review or thesis | Keep the author's organizing logic and disciplinary register; improve transitions and terminology consistency. | Do not flatten distinctions into generic prose. |
| NSFC/grant | Make the chain phenomenon/need → evidence gap → hypothesis → key scientific question → objectives/plan → feasibility visible. Make innovation testable and comparative. | Do not invent preliminary data, novelty, or expected effect sizes. |

When a journal or funding body is named, its supplied author instructions outrank these
defaults. If no section can be inferred from a short passage, polish at sentence and paragraph
level without announcing a guessed classification.
